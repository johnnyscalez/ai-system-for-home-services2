# Flooring VSL, 90-second cut

Cut from `flooring-booking-vsl.md` (Jonathan, 2026-10-28: "short, not over 1.5 minutes").
Structure kept from hormozi-vsl: hook and call-out → pain → proof → promise and method → one objection → one CTA.
About 215 spoken words, about 85 seconds at a relaxed pace. Every claim is sourced in the long version.

One big idea: **you do better work, you just don't own how clients find you. This answers first, and it's yours.**

## Script

**Hook (0 to 8 s)**
You do better floors than the company that just won your job.
They just called the homeowner first.
Flooring owners with your own crews, this is for you.

**Pain (8 to 22 s)**
You live on referrals. Busy months, then dead ones.
So you buy leads, and the same lead goes to five other companies.
By the time you call back from the job site, someone else booked the measure.

**Proof (22 to 40 s)**
And speed decides it.
Almost four in ten homeowners won't hire a contractor who misses their first call.
Reach them in five minutes, not thirty, and your odds of qualifying them jump twenty one times.

**Promise and method (40 to 70 s)**
So we built the Client Flow System.
Twenty plus in-home measures in sixty days. Guaranteed.
We run the ads, so the leads are yours. Not shared.
Every homeowner gets answered in one second, day or night.
Qualified on the floor, the rooms, the budget, and who'll be home.
Booked on your calendar. Your team notified.
And every quote chased until it signs or says no.

**Real proof (70 to 78 s)**
For a home services company we built it for: a hundred and thirty leads, thirteen booked, in nine days.

**CTA (78 to 90 s)**
Pick a time below. In twenty minutes, I'll show you how your twenty measures get booked.
I install every one myself, so I only take a few a month.
