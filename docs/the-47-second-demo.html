The 5 questions · engineered with intent
Questions That Engineer Conviction
Each question has ONE job. If you ask them in order, by question 5 the prospect is not evaluating your offer. They're announcing their own conclusion.
1
Pain Memory Access
"When's the last time you had a crew ready to go and nothing on the calendar for them? What did that week look like?"
Forces them to access a specific memory, not give a generic answer. A specific memory brings back the feeling of that week: payroll going out, crews asking what's next, the owner making calls instead of running the business. You are not creating the pain. You're making existing pain vivid.
→ If they can't recall: "What about the last time you drove 45 minutes to a measure and the spouse wasn't there?" (Everyone has that story.)
2
Invisible Loss Reveal
"How many quotes went out last month that you never heard back on? Not a no. Just quiet."
"Just quiet" is the key phrase. It opens the category of invisible losses: measures they already paid for in drive time and estimator hours that died in a "let me think about it" pile. Most owners know the jobs they lost. They've never counted the ones that faded.
→ Follow up: "And what's a typical install worth for you, rough number?" → Now you have the number. Don't calculate it yet. Just hold it.
3
Permission Bridge
"What if I could show you something that gets a homeowner from your ad to a booked measure, with everyone who decides at home, while you're on a job site? And then keeps chasing the quote so it doesn't go quiet. Worth 5 minutes?"
This is NOT a feature pitch. It's a pre-commitment question. By answering "yes" they've told their own brain "I'm going to say yes if it delivers on that." You've set the standard. The demo now just has to meet it.
4
Post-Demo · Future Pacing
"What would your next 60 days look like if every homeowner who filled out a form got that exact response, and every quote got chased like that?"
Asked AFTER they've experienced the demo, while they're still in it. This question makes them project themselves into a calendar that's already full. They're not evaluating a purchase. They're imagining their crews booked out.
→ Let them answer fully. Don't rush to the offer. This is worth 60 seconds of silence.
5
Calculator Moment · Their Number
"You said about [X] quotes a month go quiet. At [their typical install], what does that add up to? I want you to say the number."
YOU know the answer. But when THEY calculate it and SAY it, it becomes their number, not yours. "I said it, therefore it's real." Use only the numbers they give you. Never fill in a number for them.

The 7 Moves · Part 1
Moves 1 to 3 · Pain → Quantify → Permission
The First Half
The first three moves have one job: get them to feel the cost of a thin calendar and quiet quotes so clearly that the demo feels like relief, not a pitch.
1
The Pain Access Open
2 minutes · No pitch · No demo · Just listen
Activate the feeling

Start here. Not with "let me tell you what we built." Open by making them access a real memory. Their own story will do the selling for you.

"Hey [Name], really glad you jumped on. Before I show you anything, I want to ask you something that'll make the demo a lot more relevant. Can I?" [They say yes.] "When's the last time you had a crew ready and not enough measures turning into installs to keep them busy? What happened that week?"
Why this works: You're opening with curiosity and a specific memory request. The prospect relaxes because you're asking, not selling. Their answer almost always names the exact pain: thin calendar, shared leads that never pick up, measures that go nowhere. In their words, which you'll mirror back later.
Listen for their exact language. Write it down. "Angi leads are garbage" · "drove out there and the wife wasn't home" · "sent the quote and never heard back." These phrases go back into the conversation later and bring the feeling back.

Natural follow-up (read the room, use one):

  • "How many of those Angi or HomeAdvisor leads actually pick up when you call?"
  • "When a homeowner fills out your form at 9pm, who gets back to them, and when?"
  • "What does January usually look like for your crews?"
2
The Invisible Money Question
2 minutes · Get the number · Hold it
Make the loss real

Don't calculate for them. Guide them to calculate for themselves. The number they say out loud lands harder than any number you tell them.

"Okay. So last month, how many quotes went out that just went quiet? Not a no, just nothing. Rough number." [They answer.] "And a typical install for you, what does that run? Ballpark is fine." [They answer.] "So [their quotes] times [their install], what does that add up to?"
The math moment: Let them do it. When THEY say the number, it's theirs. You can reference it for the rest of the call as "your number." Not a stat. Their number. Never suggest a figure for them.
After they say the number: pause 3 full seconds. Let it sit. Don't rush to "well, that's why we built this." The silence gives the number room to land.
3
The Permission Bridge
30 seconds · Pre-commitment · No resistance
Install the yes before the demo

Before you show anything, get a conditional yes. This one sentence turns the demo from "let me pitch you" into "I'm just confirming what you already said you wanted."

"What if I could show you something that answers a homeowner in seconds, at 9pm, while you're on a job site, checks that the right people will be home, books the measure on your estimator's calendar, and then keeps chasing the quote until they sign or say no. Worth 5 minutes?"
Why this works: You haven't pitched a feature. You've described their outcome. By answering yes, they've committed to "if it does that, I'm in." The demo just needs to deliver.
Then say: "Okay. Grab your phone. I'm going to do something a little different."

Step by Step · What You Say and What They See

Step 1 · Hand them the form
"Okay. Pretend you just saw a Facebook ad from a flooring company near you, and you've been wanting to get the carpet out of the bedrooms. I'm sending you the form now. Pick LVP, say three bedrooms, put in a real ZIP and your cell. Don't overthink it. Just submit."
You do: Share your screen to the dashboard, which is currently empty. They can see it. You say nothing else. Just: "Go ahead, hit submit."
Step 2 · The short silence

You say nothing. They look at their phone. You look at the clock. In our example run it took 47 seconds; your run will land somewhere in the same range of seconds. Every second that passes, they're thinking "how long does a homeowner wait on me right now?" And then their phone buzzes.

"Oh, there it is." [Point at their phone, don't explain the system yet.] "Go ahead and reply like you're the homeowner."
Step 3 · The conversation unfolds

The AI says up front that it's an AI assistant, then qualifies: project type, rough size and rooms, timeline, ZIP in the service area, and whether every decision maker will be home for the measure. Then it offers real measure times from the estimator's calendar. Let it run without commentary.

The quote chase

After the measure, every quote is followed up by text, call and email until the homeowner signs or says no. Example cadence:

  • Day beforeConfirmation and reminder text, with a reschedule path so the measure doesn't no-show.
  • Quote dayEmail recap of the quote from the estimator.
  • Day 2Text check-in: questions for the estimator?
  • Day 5Call. If no answer, a short text follow-up.
  • OngoingSpaced text and email touches until it signs or says no. Nothing sits in "let me think about it."

Moves 5 to 7: Build Certainty, Then Make the Offer
5
Future Pace · Let Them See the Win State
90 seconds · One question · Let them answer fully
Install ownership

They've just been the homeowner. Now ask them to picture the next 60 days with it already running.

"Quick question, and I want you to actually think about it. What would the next 60 days look like if every homeowner who filled out a form got that exact response, showed up to the measure with everyone home, and every quote got chased until they signed or said no? What changes for your crews?"
Why this works: You're not asking "do you like it." You're asking them to describe their own future. They're not buying a system. They're buying the calendar they just imagined. Let them paint it.
Listen for "I wouldn't have to" or "I could finally." That's where they cross from evaluating to wanting. From here your only job is to not get in the way.
6
The Math They Build · Their Number
2 minutes · Calculator · They type it
Certainty through math

You got their quiet quotes and typical install in Move 2. Now bring it back, and add the measures. Use a visible calculator and have them say the number.

"So if you had 20 more in-home measures over the next 60 days, and you closed them at your normal rate, how many installs is that? At [their typical install], what does that come to?" [Pause. They calculate.] "And that's before we count the quotes that go quiet today and get chased instead."
Use their close rate and their install value only. Never suggest a close rate or a job value. If they don't know their close rate, ask them to guess low. A conservative number they believe beats a big one they don't.
Visual trick: Pull up the calculator on screen. Type in their numbers, let them confirm each one. The math feels collaborative and real, not a vendor deck with favorable numbers.
7
The Offer
2 minutes · The guarantee · How it works · Next step
Make yes the obvious move

Every step has been building to this. State the offer plainly, walk the five steps once, and ask for the decision.

"Here's what we do. Guaranteed 20 in-home measures in 60 days, and every quote chased until it signs or says no.

We run the ads to homeowners in your service area. You pay the ad spend straight to Meta, separate from our fee. Every lead gets answered in seconds, day or night, by text and phone. It's qualified before it touches your calendar: project, size, timeline, ZIP, and everyone who decides is home. It gets booked as an in-home measure on your estimator's real availability, with a reminder the day before. And after the measure, every quote gets chased by text, call and email.

We install it, and it plugs into the CRM or calendar you already use. You just saw what your homeowners would experience. You just built the math. Do you want that running for your crews?"
Walk through pricing live on the call, after the offer has landed. Don't put numbers in writing ahead of the conversation, and don't promise terms beyond the offer as written.

3 Rules for Move 4 (The Moment)

Rule 1: Dashboard must be empty when the call starts. Their card appearing as the only card is the visual.

Rule 2: Don't explain what's happening while it's happening. Let the AI perform without narration.

Rule 3: After "I didn't touch anything," 10 seconds of silence. Count them in your head. Then show the reminder and quote chase.
